1 · The Narrative "Why"

Five questions, asked verbatim in the submission form.

Q1What are you building?

On-chain receivable financing. A supplier uploads a signed e-invoice; the contract writes its unique document identifier and refuses a second registration of the same one. The buyer acknowledges the debt on chain. From that moment the receivable is an instrument anyone holding USDC can fund — in seconds, from fifty dollars up — and the supplier is paid the same day in their own currency through a SEP-6 anchor.

Zincir üstü alacak finansmanı. Tedarikçi imzalı e-faturasını yüklüyor; kontrat faturanın tekil kimliğini yazıyor ve aynısının ikinci kez kaydedilmesini reddediyor. Alıcı borcu zincir üstünde onaylıyor. O andan itibaren alacak, USDC tutan herkesin saniyeler içinde ve elli dolardan başlayarak fonlayabildiği bir enstrüman oluyor; tedarikçiye aynı gün kendi para biriminde, SEP-6 anchor üzerinden ödeniyor.

Q2What problem does it solve?

A supplier waits 60 to 120 days to be paid while the goods have shipped and the wages are paid. The capital that could bridge that gap is bank capital: a factor can only advance what its own balance sheet allows, and that balance sheet is bounded by the country it is licensed in. A funder abroad who would take that risk at that price cannot reach it — no local-currency account, no correspondent banking, no ticket size small enough to be worth the paperwork. Two more problems follow: the discount is asserted rather than derived, and the same receivable can be sold twice.

Tedarikçi malı sevk etmiş, maaşı ödemiş; parayı 60-120 gün bekliyor. O açığı kapatacak sermaye banka sermayesi: bir faktoring şirketi ancak kendi bilançosunun izin verdiği kadar avans verebiliyor ve o bilanço, lisanslı olduğu ülkeyle sınırlı. Bu riski o fiyata almaya razı yabancı bir fonlayıcı ise erişemiyor — yerel para hesabı yok, muhabir bankacılık yok, evrak masrafına değecek kadar küçük bir dilim yok. İki sorun daha bunu izliyor: iskonto türetilmiyor, dayatılıyor; ve aynı alacak iki kez satılabiliyor.

Q3Who are your target users?

Suppliers invoicing a single large corporate buyer — organised retail, automotive sub-industry, construction materials. Ticket sizes of roughly 1,500 to 15,000 USD equivalent on 30 to 120 day terms. On the other side, anyone holding USDC who wants short-dated, buyer-acknowledged paper. The buyer's acknowledgement is both the lock that makes the receivable real to a funder and the distribution channel: one corporate buyer brings its suppliers with it.

Tek büyük kurumsal alıcıya fatura kesen tedarikçiler — organize perakende, otomotiv yan sanayi, inşaat malzemesi. 30-120 gün vadede, yaklaşık 1.500-15.000 USD karşılığı dilimler. Diğer tarafta, kısa vadeli ve alıcısı onaylı kâğıt arayan, USDC tutan herkes. Alıcının onayı hem alacağı fonlayıcı gözünde gerçek kılan kilit hem de dağıtım kanalı: bir kurumsal alıcı, tedarikçilerini beraberinde getiriyor.

Q4Why is this problem worth solving?

The market is established rather than speculative — it already pays. In the first corridor the figures are public: Türkiye had 4,016,059 active enterprises in 2025, of which 99.6% are SMEs (TÜİK), and the factoring sector turned over 1.875 trillion TRY across roughly 95,000 customers in 2025. What that market does not have is a price anyone can check, or capital from outside the licensing perimeter. Both are addressable.

Pazar spekülatif değil, kurulu — ve zaten ödüyor. İlk koridorda rakamlar açık: Türkiye'de 2025'te 4.016.059 aktif girişim vardı, bunların %99,6'sı KOBİ (TÜİK); faktoring sektörü 2025'te yaklaşık 95.000 müşteri üzerinden 1,875 trilyon TL hacim döndürdü. Bu pazarda olmayan şey, herkesin kontrol edebileceği bir fiyat ve lisans çevresinin dışından gelen sermaye. İkisi de çözülebilir.

Tedarikçi aynı gün, kendi para biriminde, normal banka hesabına ödeme alıyor ve kriptoya hiç dokunmuyor. Fonlayıcı kapalı bir pazara elli dolardan, o ülkede hesap açmadan erişiyor. Alıcı, kendi kredisini uzatmadan tedarikçisini likit tutuyor. Herkes için: iskonto zincir üstü veriden hesaplanıyor ve her bileşen kaynağıyla etiketli.

The mechanism is not country-bound

The shape of the problem is the same wherever suppliers invoice on terms. What changes per country is the document standard, the invoice identifier and the anchor. Türkiye is the first corridor and what runs on testnet today.

Which integration is load-bearing

Without the anchor the supplier cannot be paid and the buyer cannot settle — that is the product's entire proposition. Without the treasury, fund() cannot complete and the yield component of the price has no source. Neither is decoration.

TR · Anchor olmadan tedarikçi ödenemiyor ve alıcı kapatamıyor — ürünün bütün önerisi bu. Hazine olmadan fund() tamamlanamıyor ve fiyatın getiri bileşeninin kaynağı kalmıyor. İkisi de süs değil.
T4Key design decisions and trade-offs
DecisionWhat it costs, and why we took it
The identifier is the invariantUniqueness is a contract rule, not a database row. A licensed factor inside a country with a registry can already check one; a funder abroad cannot. The contract gives them the same guarantee without asking them to join anything.
Every price component carries its sourceMore surface to build and to explain, but a number that fell back to a parameter cannot arrive wearing the live badge. This is the decision the rest of the product is organised around.
SEP-6 rather than SEP-24SEP-24 hands the flow to the anchor's own hosted interface. SEP-6 is API-first, which is what lets us show every request and status in the open. The cost is that we implement the flow ourselves.
The treasury is an interface, not a vaultThe invoice contract knows four functions and cannot tell which treasury is behind them. That is why a DeFindex outage or a swap does not touch the flow.
Yield falls through, never substitutesThe vault's realised gain first, a Blend pool's lending rate second, and a refusal third. It would have been easier to return the configured number and call it live.
Keys live in route handlersThree parties on one laptop makes the demo legible, and a passkey cannot sign Soroban XDR on its own yet. The cost is real and it is stated in the limitations: this is the thing a production deployment must change first.

Kimliğin değişmez olması, her fiyat bileşeninin kaynağını taşıması, SEP-24 yerine SEP-6, hazinenin kasa değil arayüz olması, getirinin sayı uydurmak yerine reddetmesi, ve anahtarların sunucuda durması. Sonuncusu gerçek bir maliyet ve sınırlılıklar bölümünde açıkça yazılı — üretimde ilk değişmesi gereken şey bu.

T5Technical challenges and how we solved them
ChallengeResolution
Cross-contract authorisationThe treasury moves the token one frame deeper than the invoker's signature reaches. authorize_as_current_contract() authorises that sub-call only, for that amount only.
The DeFindex vault pulls with transfer, not transfer_fromThe first adapter granted an allowance and came back with Auth, InvalidAction. We authorise the vault's pull explicitly with the same pattern. Deposit and withdraw verified on testnet with real funds.
The anchor names its memo typeWe sent MEMO_TEXT where it asked for MEMO_ID. The payment arrived at the treasury and the transfer sat at pending_user_transfer_start for ever. A simulated rail would not have cared — this bug is the proof the rail is real.
Anchor transfer limits3,000 ₺ per transfer, 50 ₺ minimum. The splitter broke twice: greedy filling left a remainder under the minimum, even division piled it onto the last part and crossed the cap. The version that rounds parts up and leaves the shortfall last was swept across 1,823 amounts.
Asset naming differs between SEP-38 and SEP-6SEP-38 wants stellar:USDC:ISSUER and iso4217:TRY; SEP-6's exchange endpoints want the bare code for the asset but the ISO form for fiat. Found through three separate 400s.
An hour of lending rounds to nothingDividing to basis points before scaling to a year truncated the whole measurement to zero, and the yield reading silently disappeared. annualise_bps multiplies first; four tests hold it against two real readings of the pool taken eighty-five minutes apart.
An unbounded funding ledgerFound by running the official Stellar security checklist. repay() walks the ledger and transfer_claim() rebuilds it, so enough entries would strand an invoice. Closed with a minimum ticket and a cap of 32 entries, held by three tests.
Audio on iOSFour rounds: a suspended AudioContext, a carrier above the voice-band roll-off, a preamble that recurred inside payloads, and a room's reverb merging bursts. The context is now created before the first await, and the detection threshold adapts to a measured noise floor.
Next.js route handler isolationRoute handlers are bundled separately and share no module state. Challenges, the rate limiter and the session secret live on globalThis; payout accounts and passkeys are written to disk atomically, so a deploy does not reset the demo.

Kontratlar arası yetkilendirme, DeFindex'in transfer kullanması, anchor'ın memo tipi, transfer limitleri, SEP-38 ile SEP-6 arasındaki varlık adlandırma farkı, bir saatlik getirinin sıfıra yuvarlanması, sınırsız fonlama defteri, iOS'ta ses ve route handler yalıtımı. Dokuzunun da çözümü yukarıda; memo tipi hatası özellikle önemli, çünkü simüle bir ray bunu umursamazdı.

What the security review found — and we fixed

Every privileged entry point authorises from stored config, init refuses a second call, storage keys are typed, arithmetic is checked, error codes are appended rather than renumbered, and events cover state changes. One item did not pass: "loops and footprints bounded on attacker-shaped input." fund() accepted any amount above zero and appended to an unbounded ledger that repay() walks. Closed with a configurable minimum ticket — waived only for the contribution that closes an invoice, so the floor can never strand one — and a cap of 32 entries. Three tests hold it.

The problem is defined by a number a supplier can read off their own ageing report: sixty to a hundred and twenty days. The use case is trade finance and local payments, both named in the brief. Who benefits: suppliers who stay solvent without a bank line, and funders who reach a market that was closed to them. The target segment is specific — suppliers invoicing one large corporate buyer, 1,500 to 15,000 USD per invoice — which is also the distribution channel, because one buyer brings its suppliers.

Problem, tedarikçinin kendi vade raporundan okuyabileceği bir sayıyla tanımlı: 60-120 gün. Kullanım alanı ticaret finansmanı ve yerel ödemeler, ikisi de şartnamede geçiyor. Kazanan: banka limiti olmadan ayakta kalan tedarikçi ve kendisine kapalı bir pazara erişen fonlayıcı. Hedef kitle belirli ve aynı zamanda dağıtım kanalı.

8 · Honest limitations

Stated here rather than found later

Nobody bears the currency risk yet. The funder's claim is fixed in USDC while the buyer owes a fixed amount of local currency. Over ninety days those stop matching, and the contract does not say who absorbs the difference. The currency premium prices that risk; it does not assign it.

The funder's claim has no legal wrapper. The contract creates a pro-rata economic interest in a payout, not an assignment of the receivable. What that is under securities law differs by country.

A fabricated invoice passes our checks. We verify structure, not existence. The gate is acknowledge() — the address written on the invoice must sign. What that does not stop is collusion between a supplier and a buyer.

Also: the signature is checked structurally, not against the tax authority's certificate chain; the bank leg runs against a sandbox anchor; we publish the testnet price feed ourselves; and funding transactions are signed server-side until a passkey smart account verifies secp256r1 on chain.
